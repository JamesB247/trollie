# Target market: who Trollie is for, and in what order

Research for the founders, 29 October 2026. Not yet adopted into the proposal or the PRD.
Figures come from National Treasury, the South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Stats SA, the
Pietermaritzburg Economic Justice & Dignity group (PMBEJD), Shoprite's FY2026 results and
press coverage; secondary sources are marked.

| Household debt to disposable income | 62.2% in Q1 2026, up from 61.8% | SARB Quarterly Bulletin, June 2026 |

**What this means for Trollie.** Most households are squeezed: high unemployment, rising debt,
flat spending and a food basket that takes a large share of income. For the mass market, the
promise has to be *saving money and time*, and household fees have to be close to zero. Only the
upper segment can pay a service fee or subscription comfortably.

## 2. Digital readiness

- 23.1% of working-age South Africans used generative AI in Q1 2026; first in Africa
  (Microsoft AI Diffusion Report).
- Online shoppers cluster in households earning R50,000+ a month (67.8%) and ages 25–34 (39.5%)
  (Peach Payments 2026 Online Retail Report).
- Only 23% of South Africans would trust an AI agent to buy for them today (Visa, 2026).
- Home languages: isiZulu 24.4%, isiXhosa 16.3%, Afrikaans 10.6%, Sepedi 10%, English 8.7%
  (Census 2022). Speech technology for South African languages exists but is young. Lelapa AI's
  Vulavula supports isiZulu, Sesotho, Afrikaans and South African English; Setswana is not listed,
  and no accuracy figures are published.
- 1 GB of mobile data costs about R20.50 on average (2025), which weighs on prepaid users.

## 4. Segments for Trollie, and the brand each one maps to

| # | Segment | Maps to | Size signal | Why they would use Trollie | Can they pay a fee? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Time-poor digital households**: working parents, professionals, early AI adopters | Checkers, Checkers Hyper, Sixty60 | Online shoppers cluster in R50k+ households | Whole-list shopping in a conversation; standing instructions; their own agents later | Yes: service fee or subscription |
| B | **Stokvels and bulk buyers** | Shoprite Cash & Carry, Red Star, Checkers Hyper | Over 11 million members in about 810,000 groups, R45–50 billion a year; grocery stokvels most common | The organiser's time, transport and order-splitting are the pain | Small fee per group order; large baskets carry it |
| C | **Value households**: mid to lower income, voice-first, many languages | Shoprite brand | Shoprite and Usave: R121.6 billion of sales | Speak an order in isiZulu or Sesotho; month-end and payday shopping; saves a trip | Little or none; funded by retailer commission and bulk margins |
| D | **Grant-dependent households** | Usave, Shoprite | 26.5 million grant beneficiaries | Saves transport and time, but small baskets | No; only viable through group orders and collection points |
| E | **Spaza and informal traders** (later, B2B) | Shoprite Cash & Carry, OK Franchise | Not sized here | Restocking without losing a day | Yes, as a business tool |

## 5. Recommended sequence

1. **Pilot: segments A and B together**, in one Gauteng area served by a Checkers, a Shoprite and
   a Shoprite Cash & Carry. For example, about 50 digital households plus 3–5 stokvels.
   - Voice in English and isiZulu from day one, with Sesotho and Setswana tested.
   - A has the best margins; B brings large baskets and the township bridge, and uses Cash & Carry.
2. **Phase 2: segment C**, the Shoprite brand's value customers.
   - Voice-first in their own languages, with read-back before every order.
   - Collection at a nearby store or spaza shop instead of door delivery.
   - Balances funded through a channel they already trust, such as the Money Market account
     (to discuss with the Group).
   - Households pay little or nothing; revenue comes from retailer commission and bulk margins.
3. **Phase 3: segments D and E**, only where group ordering and collection make the numbers work.
   This is also a strong social-impact story for partners and funders.

## 6. What it changes in the pitch to Shoprite

- **Sixty60 already serves the affluent online cohort.** Trollie's distinct value to the Group is:
  - bringing *agent and voice ordering* to the Shoprite brand's value customers, in their own
    languages;
  - bringing stokvel and bulk demand into Cash & Carry.
- **Pricing differs by segment:** service fee or subscription for segment A; near-free for C and
  D, funded by retailer commission.
- **The Money Market account and Xtra Savings** are natural links for the mass market.
